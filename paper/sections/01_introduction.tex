\section{Introduction}

Diffusion language models expose intermediate refinement states before emitting
a final sequence. A failed final answer alone does not establish whether an
earlier state supported recovery. We study the question: when a sampled
reasoning trajectory ends incorrectly, can a specified intervention recover a
correct answer from one of its intermediate states?

We distinguish observed transient correctness, stochastic continuation
recoverability, and intervention-conditioned recoverability. Their differences
matter: a state that already admits recovery under resampled native continuation
does not by itself establish an intervention benefit. Recovery estimates must
also use confirmation samples independent of checkpoint localization.

The reference-primary design first validates a source-native decoder and its
benchmark bridge, then checks that snapshot instrumentation preserves the exact
trajectory. Repairability is measured on the same sampled trajectory bank. This
validation chain is a prerequisite for interpreting the experiments; it is not
a separate headline research question.

The four research questions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item RQ1: Do failed reference-quality trajectories contain confirmed repairable states?
\item RQ2: How much recovery is attributable to native stochastic continuation
versus intervention lift?
\item RQ3: Can observable state signals prospectively localize useful interventions,
including their harm to successful trajectories?
\item RQ4: How do these quantities vary across the declared tasks and backbones?
\end{enumerate}
This draft specifies the measurement and reporting structure. It makes no
numerical reference-primary claim before the corresponding artifacts are sealed.
